Cap rates explained for first-time investors

How to calculate a cap rate, what it tells you, and what it doesn’t.

5 min read · Updated September 2026 · HomeQuest Commercial

The formula

Cap rate = net operating income ÷ price. A building earning $271,600 a year after running costs, priced at $4,850,000, has a cap rate of 5.6%.

What it tells you

The cap rate is the yearly return you’d earn if you paid cash. It lets you compare properties of different sizes and prices on the same scale.

What it doesn’t tell you

  • How secure the income is: a 7% cap rate with a tenant leaving next year can be worse than 5% with long leases
  • Upcoming costs, such as a new roof
  • Your return after a mortgage
  • Whether rents are below market and could rise

Check the numbers yourself

Ask for the rent roll, the leases and two to three years of operating expenses. Recalculate NOI with realistic vacancy and management costs; some listings leave these out.

Financing

Lenders look at how comfortably the income covers the mortgage payments. Expect to need 25–35% down. Each investment listing on this site shows the estimated income left after a mortgage. See investment properties for sale.

Common questions

Is a higher cap rate better?

It means more income for the price, but usually comes with more risk, such as shorter leases, weaker tenants or an older building.
