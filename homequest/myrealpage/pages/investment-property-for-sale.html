Investment property for sale: what to know

Investment properties already have tenants paying rent. They are compared by cap rate (the yearly income as a percentage of the price), the strength of the tenants and how long their leases have left.

What to check before you buy

  • Net operating income, with the rent roll and expenses
  • Who the tenants are, and how long their leases run
  • Which costs tenants pay and which the owner pays
  • Building condition and upcoming repairs
  • The risk if a major tenant leaves
  • Financing: the down payment, and how well the income covers the mortgage

FAQ

Questions about investment property for sale

Don’t see yours? Ask us directly.

What is a good cap rate?

There isn’t one right number. Lower cap rates usually mean safer tenants or better locations; higher cap rates pay more income but may carry more risk. Compare cap rates between similar properties in similar areas.

Can I finance an investment property?

Yes. Lenders usually want a down payment of 25–35% and will check that the rental income comfortably covers the mortgage payments.

How much down payment do I need for commercial property?

Lenders usually ask for 25–35% of the price. Businesses buying their own premises may qualify for less through programs such as BDC.

What costs are there on top of the price?

Plan for property transfer tax (1% of the first $200,000 and 2% of the rest for commercial property), 5% GST unless you are GST-registered, legal fees, inspections and an appraisal.

How long does it take to buy a commercial property?

Usually 60 to 120 days from accepted offer to completion, including 30 to 60 days of due diligence.
