Commercial real estate in Coquitlam: what to know

Coquitlam’s commercial core is around Coquitlam Centre and the Millennium Line stations at Lincoln and Coquitlam Central. Burquitlam and the Lougheed corridor are adding new mixed-use buildings, and United Boulevard is a long-established area for industrial and large-format retail.

Where to look in Coquitlam

City CentreCoquitlam Centre, offices and new towers near the Millennium Line.
BurquitlamA fast-growing area around Burquitlam station, with new shops at street level.
Austin HeightsNeighbourhood shopping and services along Austin Avenue.
MaillardvilleA historic area along Brunette Avenue with small shops and services.
United BoulevardIndustrial and large-format retail near Highway 1 and the Fraser River.

Getting around: Highway 1, the Lougheed Highway and the Mary Hill Bypass, and the SkyTrain Millennium Line.

Questions about commercial real estate in Coquitlam

Don’t see yours? Ask us directly.

Is Coquitlam good for an auto repair or trades business?

Yes. United Boulevard and the industrial areas near the Fraser River have zoning for repair shops and trades, with good access to Highway 1.

Where is new retail space in Coquitlam?

Most new space is at street level in mixed-use buildings around the SkyTrain stations, especially in Burquitlam and City Centre.

How much does it cost to lease commercial space?

Commercial rent is quoted per square foot per year, in two parts: base rent and additional rent (taxes, insurance and upkeep). Add them, multiply by the size and divide by 12, then add 5% GST. We show this monthly estimate on every listing.

How much down payment do I need for commercial property?

Lenders usually ask for 25–35% of the price. Businesses buying their own premises may qualify for less through programs such as BDC.
