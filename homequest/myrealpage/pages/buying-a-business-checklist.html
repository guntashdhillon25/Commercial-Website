Buying a business

Buying a small business: a practical checklist

How businesses are priced, what to check, and how the process works from NDA to closing.

7 min read · Updated September 2026 · HomeQuest Commercial

How small businesses are priced

Most small businesses are priced on owner’s cash flow (SDE): what the business earns for one full-time owner before loans and taxes. Asking prices are often 1.5 to 3 times SDE, depending on the industry, how steady the income is and how much depends on the current owner.

Start with the confidentiality agreement

Owners keep sales private, so you’ll sign a short confidentiality agreement before you see the name, address and financial statements. It doesn’t commit you to buy. See all businesses for sale.

What to check

  • At least three years of financial statements and tax returns
  • How SDE was calculated, and which expenses were added back
  • The lease: time left, renewal options and whether it can be transferred
  • Staff: who is key, and whether they plan to stay
  • Customers: whether a few large ones make up most of the sales
  • Equipment condition, and what the price includes
  • Licences and permits that need to be transferred

Asset sale or share sale

In an asset sale, you buy the business’s equipment, brand and customer list, usually through a new company. In a share sale, you buy the company itself, including its history. Asset sales are more common for small businesses. Your accountant and lawyer can advise which suits you.

Paying for it

Buyers often combine savings with a bank or BDC loan. Some sellers accept part of the price over time (vendor financing), which also shows they believe in the business. See how financing works.

From offer to closing

Your offer is usually subject to due diligence, financing and the landlord agreeing to transfer the lease. After closing, most sellers train the new owner for a few weeks.

Common questions

How much money do I need to buy a small business?

Lenders usually expect you to put in 20–50% of the price from your own funds, plus working capital for the first few months.

Will the seller train me?

Most do. Two to eight weeks of training is common, and it’s usually written into the purchase agreement.

Have a question about your situation?

An advisor can answer it, usually the same day.
