Should your business lease or buy its space?

The trade-offs between leasing and owning, and a quick way to compare them.

5 min read · Updated September 2026 · HomeQuest Commercial

Leasing: flexibility and lower upfront cost

Leasing keeps your cash in the business and makes it easier to move or grow. It’s the usual choice for new businesses and for anyone who isn’t sure where they’ll be in 5 to 10 years. Browse space for lease.

Buying: control and equity

Owning your space means no landlord, no rent increases and a property that can gain value over time. It suits established businesses that plan to stay put. Browse property for sale.

A quick comparison

Compare your total monthly rent with the monthly cost of owning: mortgage payment, property taxes, insurance, maintenance and any strata fees. Then consider the down payment (typically 25–35%) and what that cash could earn if you kept it in the business instead.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Will I need the same size and location in 10 years?
  • Can I make the down payment without starving the business of cash?
  • Would I rather spend time running the business or managing a building?
  • Could I rent part of the space to another business?
