Buying in New Westminster

Businesses for sale in New Westminster: what to know

New Westminster is one of the region’s oldest cities. It is small but central, with shops and restaurants on Columbia Street, Uptown around Sixth Street, and medical offices near Royal Columbian Hospital in Sapperton. Queensborough, across the river, has industrial land and a shopping centre.

Buying a business means taking over a working operation: customers, staff, equipment, suppliers and usually the lease. Most small businesses are priced on what they earn for their owner each year.

Where to look in New Westminster

DowntownHeritage storefronts on Columbia Street, the Quay and New Westminster station.
UptownShopping and services around Sixth Street and Sixth Avenue.
SappertonMedical and professional offices near Royal Columbian Hospital.
QueensboroughIndustrial land and Queensborough Landing shopping centre.

Getting around: Five SkyTrain Expo Line stations and Highway 91A, next to Burnaby, Surrey and Coquitlam.

What to check before you buy

  • Owner’s cash flow (SDE), and how it was calculated
  • Three years of financial statements and tax returns
  • Time left on the lease, and whether it can be transferred to you
  • Whether key staff and customers are likely to stay
  • What’s included: equipment, inventory, brand and website
  • How much training the seller will give

FAQ

Questions about businesses for sale in New Westminster

Don’t see yours? Ask us directly.

Is New Westminster good for a medical office?

Yes, especially in Sapperton near Royal Columbian Hospital, where clinics and specialists cluster.

Are there heritage buildings for sale in New Westminster?

Downtown has many older buildings with shops on the ground floor and apartments or offices above. Check for heritage status, which can limit changes to the outside of the building.

Why are business listings confidential?

Owners don’t want staff, customers or competitors to find out before a sale is agreed. You sign a short confidentiality agreement (NDA) to see the name, address and financial statements.

How are small businesses valued?

Usually as a multiple of the owner’s yearly cash flow (SDE), often between 1.5 and 3 times, depending on the industry, how steady the income is and how much depends on the owner.
