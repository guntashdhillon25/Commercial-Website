Net vs. gross leases, explained

Two ways landlords charge for building costs, and how to compare them.

4 min read · Updated September 2026 · HomeQuest Commercial

Net lease

In a net lease, you pay base rent plus your share of the building’s costs as additional rent. It’s the most common type for retail, industrial and many offices in BC. You may also see “triple net” or “NNN”, meaning taxes, insurance and maintenance are all passed on to tenants.

Gross lease

In a gross lease, one rent covers the space and the building’s costs. It’s simpler to budget for, and more common for small offices, medical suites and shared buildings.

How to compare them

Convert both to a total monthly cost. A net lease at $30 plus $14 additional rent costs about the same as a gross lease at $44. Then look at what happens over time: with a net lease you carry the risk of rising taxes and costs; with a gross lease the landlord does, and prices that into the rent.

Questions to ask

  • What exactly is included in additional rent?
  • What was last year’s actual additional rent?
  • Is there a cap on management fee increases?
  • Who pays for major repairs, such as the roof or heating system?
