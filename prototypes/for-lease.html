Glossary

Lease terms, in plain English

Base rent
What the landlord charges for the space itself, per square foot per year.
Additional rent
Your share of property tax, insurance and upkeep. Also called CAM or operating costs.
Triple net (NNN)
A lease where you pay base rent plus additional rent. The most common type.
TI allowance
Money the landlord puts toward your build-out, usually quoted per square foot.
Personal guarantee
You personally promise the rent if your business can’t pay. Often limited in time or amount.
Assignment
Transferring your lease to someone else, for example when you sell your business.
FAQ

Commercial leasing questions I get asked most

Don’t see yours? Ask me directly.

How much does it cost to lease commercial space?

Commercial rent is quoted per square foot per year, in two parts: base rent (the landlord’s income) and additional rent (property tax, insurance and building maintenance). Add them together, multiply by the size and divide by 12 for a monthly figure. For example, 1,850 sq ft at $32 base + $14 additional = $46/sf/yr, or about $7,092 a month before GST.

What does triple net (NNN) mean?

In a triple net lease, you pay base rent plus your share of the building’s property taxes, insurance and maintenance. That share is the “additional rent” on each listing. In a gross lease, those costs are included in one rent figure.

Do I pay GST on commercial rent?

Yes. In Canada, GST applies to commercial rent. If your business is GST-registered you can usually claim it back as an input tax credit. Check with your accountant.

How long is a typical commercial lease?

Most retail and office leases run 3 to 10 years, often with an option to renew. Restaurants usually need 10 years or more to make their build-out cost worthwhile. Small industrial and flex units can be shorter.

What do I need to lease a space?

Landlords usually ask for a business plan or description, recent financial statements (or personal ones for a new business), a deposit, and often a personal guarantee. I’ll help you put together an application that makes landlords take you seriously.

Who pays your fee as a tenant’s agent?

In most commercial leases, the landlord pays the leasing commission, so tenants usually don’t pay me directly. I’ll confirm how it works for each space before you commit.

Can the landlord pay for improvements?

Often, yes. Many landlords offer a tenant improvement (TI) allowance, free-rent months, or both, especially on longer leases. These are negotiable, and negotiating them is a big part of what I do for you.

How early should I start looking?

Start 6 to 12 months before you need to move in. Finding a space, negotiating, getting permits and building out often takes longer than expected, especially for restaurants.
